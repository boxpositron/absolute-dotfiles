# Herdr

Herdr's desktop app (`herdr-app`, with herdr-core as `hc`) as the window around tmux. tmux stays as it is: each Herdr tab runs a tmux client (`tma <session>`), and Herdr adds the theme, the agents list and its sounds and toasts around it.

## Files

`setup-mac.sh links` links these into `~/.config/herdr-app/`, which holds everything else Herdr writes:

- `settings.json`: Catppuccin Mocha and Latte for layouts without a theme of their own, following the system's light or dark mode; JetBrainsMono Nerd Font 14 like Ghostty; omo's own `omo-done` and `omo-input` sounds for Herdr's done and blocked alerts; telemetry off.
- `themes/catppuccin-mocha.json`, `themes/catppuccin-latte.json`: terminal colours from Catppuccin's Ghostty palettes, plus Herdr panel and status colours from the same palette (blue working, yellow needs you, green done).
- `themes/netrunner.json`: the `netrunner` layout's own cyberpunk theme (Settings offers it only there): near-black blue terminals, neon ANSI colours, hot pink accent, cyan working, Cyberpunk yellow needs you, green done. Dark only, whatever the system's mode. tmux's bar and pane borders (`.tmux.conf` at the repo root) use the same palette.
- `themes/ash.json`: the `ash` layout's own theme: dark grey terminals, muted ANSI colours, warm off-white text, a cream accent and cursor, soft cyan working, amber needs you, green done. Dark only.
- `programs.json`: omo with its own name and icon, and in the + menu to start it in a pane of its own.
- `layouts/netrunner.json`: Herdr's main layout, cloned from 0.1.13 (called `tmux` until 2026-11-11), with the "omo in tmux" panel in the side panel and the sidebar from `workspace-chrome-tmux`, in the `netrunner` theme with near-square pane corners, a purple sidebar gradient and a cyan divider. The window is frosted glass: `window_blur` blurs the desktop behind it, and the layout's colours carry alpha: the root is dark glass (a purple-to-black gradient at 50 to 60%), and the layers on it stack on that base (terminal, side panel and priority queue cards at 30%, the sidebar gradient at 10%, bars at 25%, terminals with `terminal_opacity: 0`), so the gaps between panels show the desktop dimly and the panels show it fainter still. Herdr adds the alphas of stacked layers together rather than blending them, so a panel goes opaque once its alpha plus the root's reaches 100%: keep each layer's alpha plus the root's under about 85% (a 60% root under 65% panels turned them solid). Herdr paints the theme's `background` colour under every card and terminal, so alpha there stacks into an almost opaque window: the layout keeps `colors.background` clear and puts the tint on the pane frames, the side panel's two nodes and the `queue` column instead. Theme files take no alpha, so it all lives in the layout. Modals stay solid: Herdr draws its own dialogs and popups (the picker, the palette, Add machine) on a solid frame, and the two that draw their own frame (`frame: "none"`), the omo Board and the preview's focus mode, fill with the theme's background without its alpha. Its terminal has no focus ring: a red edge down its right side (`mods/focus-edge/`) shows the keyboard is in it. The status bar's row also holds three zero-width blocks: hidden copies of `tmux-context`, `omo-tmux` and `mcp-tmux` (props `{"headless": true}`). Herdr starts a block only the first time it shows and gives keys only to shown copies, and the side panel is often closed, so these copies follow tmux, poll and take Cmd+J and the folder picker key, and publish what they find in shared values; the copies in the panel only draw it (the context strip draws `tmux.context` and loads only PRs and Linear itself). They react to change rather than poll fast: Herdr's focus, tmux's hooks (which touch `~/.local/state/omo-status/where/tick`) and omo state changes (`omo-status set` touches `.../list/tick`) start a read at once, and the timers are a safety net (`tmux-context` every 15 s, the local omo list every 5 s, the other machines' every 15 s). Herdr no longer updates a cloned layout, so changes to `main` in later releases don't reach it. Switch layouts with Cmd+Shift+L.
- `layouts/ash.json`: the `netrunner` layout in other colours: the same blocks, mods and frosted glass, on neutral grey glass instead of purple, with a 1 px cream outline round the terminal and each side panel and a cream divider. Its keys in `keys.json` and Option+click to the Files panel work as in `netrunner`. It was made from `netrunner.json` by changing only colours, so a change to netrunner's blocks has to be carried over by hand; `herdr-tmux-update` merges Herdr's changes into it with its own base, `base/ash-main.json`. Switch with Cmd+Shift+L or `herdr-app do layout.switch --name ash`.
- `mods/omo-tmux/`: the "omo in tmux" panel.
- `mods/agents-tmux/`: the sidebar's one tree of spaces and agents: Herdr's Agents list, cloned from `agents` in 0.1.15, with Herdr's Spaces list (cloned from `spaces` in 0.1.16) folded into it; the spaces' git, machines, colours and order live in `spaces.js`. Each machine (This Mac first, unless you drag another above it) has a header with its spaces, how many of their tabs need you and a + for a new space there, and for another machine its link: a dot, its state, Connect when it was rebuilt, or the login command with Copy. Under it come Herdr's spaces in Herdr's order, each on a wash of its colour (its tmux session's, as its tmux bar shows it, else the space's own) with its initial, tab count and git (the repo and branch where unusual, ahead and behind; a worktree's space sits under its checkout's), and under each space its tabs: the tab's name (in tab mode, the window's work) and the state, message and age of the omo pane in the tmux window it shows. Which window each tab shows comes from tmux's clients, matched to Herdr's terminals by pid every 3 s and whenever a client comes or goes, here and on each connected machine, and is published as shared `tmux.tabs` for the picker too. A tmux client not in tab mode lists its session's omo panes under its tab, as does a window running more than one; panes in windows whose tab you closed follow the tabs, and the omo sessions no space shows fold under "Not in Herdr". A click on a space goes to it, its chevron folds it and right-click opens its menu. Machines and spaces each have a drag handle, six neon dots in the row's left gutter (very bright yellow for machines, cyan for spaces; the whole row drags): dragging a space lifts it and its tabs onto a raised card while the machine's other spaces slide apart, and letting go moves it there (`space.move`); dragging a machine's header moves the machine with everything under it, and that order is the tree's own, kept across restarts (`machines.order`), which the rail follows too. A click on a tab goes to it. A change of state never moves a row, and rows slide to their new places whenever the order changes (160ms ease-out). Needs you is the theme's pink (a pulsing pill, the name, a bar at the row's edge), failed the same in red, so neither reads as the remote server's yellow. Machines, spaces and sessions fold, and the folds are kept. Herdr counts a tmux client as one agent carrying its session's most urgent state, so those terminals get no row; agents outside tmux follow under "Not in tmux" in Herdr's grouped or priority views, and the sort button shows only when there are some. On the rail (the collapsed sidebar, under a SPACES label), each space is a segment of its own, a rounded card washed and edged in its colour (brighter while shown) with a gap to the next, holding its tile with its tab count on the corner, its name, then a dot for each of its agents (each omo pane once, and the agent of a tab that isn't tmux), in its state's colour, wrapping into a grid six wide: pulsing while working, in the background, waiting on you or failed, solid once finished, a ring while idle. On hover the tile shows the message of the tab that most needs you; a click goes to it. The sidebar's search and toggle stay at its top either way, under the traffic lights on the macOS rail (`workspace-chrome-tmux`). Another machine is named in the Cyberpunk yellow of its tmux bar.

## omo's status in Herdr

Herdr knows an agent's state only when the program reports it with OSC 7501 (the [Program Status Protocol](https://www.superlogical.com/rex/docs/build/program-status)), and omo inside tmux can't report through tmux. `omo-status` reports for it instead. A tmux client whose environment holds `HC_TERMINAL_ID` runs in a Herdr terminal, so omo-status sends that client its session's most urgent state, from every omo pane in the session:

| omo | Herdr |
| --- | --- |
| working on a prompt | working |
| turn over, a build, watcher or child session still running, or a wake-up scheduled | working, with `◐ background · <what is running>` as the message (the protocol has no background state, and Herdr shows a terminal that goes from working to idle while you aren't looking as done) |
| asked you something | blocked (question) |
| finished, every task in its todo list closed | done |
| finished a step, with tasks still open in its todo list | done, with `Step done · 4 of 9 left · next: <task>` as the message (the protocol has no such state) |
| stopped with an error | error |
| at rest, or you've looked | idle |

"Step done" (omo-status's `partial`) comes from the session's transcript: when omo stops, `omo-status set <pane> done` reads the last `senpi.todo-state` entry, and a list with tasks still pending or in progress makes it a step, not the end of the work. The Agents tree, the omo panel and the context strip draw it as a green ring around a dot, beside finished's solid dot; the Ghostty tab marker is 🔘. A session without a todo list, or with every task closed, finishes as before.

The message names the project and the task, the first line of the prompt: `studio: Finished: fix the imap timeouts`. tmux's `client-attached` and `client-session-changed` hooks send it again when a client arrives or switches session. omo running straight in a Herdr pane, outside tmux, reports to its own terminal. Where Herdr shows the session, `omo-notify` plays no sound and posts no banner of its own, and Herdr plays omo's sounds. Ghostty tabs work as before, and Ghostty gets no reports.

## The "omo in tmux" panel

The sidebar's tree shows every pane running omo under its space and tab (see `mods/agents-tmux/`). The panel, a view of the side panel beside Files, Changes and MCP, lists the same panes with their messages on a line of their own, grouped by session: project and window name, state, and message, read with `omo-status list-local` the moment a pane's omo state changes or Herdr's focus moves (else every 5 s), and `omo-status list-remote` for the other machines the moment `omo-status follow` (a launchd agent, set up with `omo-status install`) hears one of them change, or after Seen clears one of their panes, with a read every 15 s while the agent answers and every 3 s once it has been quiet for 25 s. A click runs `omo-status show <pane>` (see Spaces).

The status bar's omo chip (`omo 9`, with a yellow dot when a pane needs you) opens and closes it. The hidden copy beside the status bar reads the list and takes Cmd+J, and publishes the list (`omo.list`) and the counts (`omo.tmux`), so the chip and Cmd+J work with the panel closed.

`omo-status list` finds omo by its process (bun running senpi's CLI) on each pane's terminal, so a pane shows as soon as omo runs in it, idle until omo's hooks report for it, and goes when omo exits. A pane running mosh or ssh shows the remote session's state, which its terminal title brought.

Inside each session the panes that need you come first, then finished, working, background and idle ones. A pane whose turn is over but whose work still runs shows a slower, purple spinner and what it is running (`Watching: staging deploy`, `Running: gh pr checks 13 (+1 more)`, `goal · waiting on CI watch`). A pane with a `schedule_prompt` job counts down to its wake-up, and a parked goal counts down too (`goal continues in 3:34 · waiting on CI watch`): omo stores that time nowhere, so `omo-status` reads it from the footer omo draws in the pane. The Agents tree counts down the same way; the status Herdr shows for the session's terminal can't, so there it reads in whole minutes and changes once a minute. Each row shows how long the pane has been in its state (`@omo_since`, which `omo-status` sets on every change). The panel moves the way herdr-t3-inbox does: a row whose place changes slides there, a new one fades in from above, one that goes fades out where it was, all over 150ms ease-out. "Needs you" and "Failed" pulse, and a pane that has just finished flashes green once and fades over 1.5s.

## Search

In the `netrunner` layout, Cmd+P and the sidebar's search button open `picker-tmux`, one search across Herdr and tmux, with the search field on top and the keys in a footer. With nothing typed it lists the spaces with something running, one row per tab with its space's tile and name (the tab you are on selected and marked "here"), folds quiet spaces into one row, then lists each machine's tmux sessions that no Herdr terminal shows yet (this Mac, then each connected machine in yellow) with their windows, folder and last activity, from `omo-status sessions`. Typing matches tabs by space, tab or agent name, sessions by name and panes by window name, program, folder or omo message, so `riga` finds the prawnwire pane working in the riga worktree; a session Herdr already shows is marked "in Herdr". Every tmux pane is searchable, not only omo's. Enter or a click on a session or pane shows it through `omo-status show`, as the "omo in tmux" panel does: the Herdr tab already on it, else a tab in the space named after it, else a new space.

The picker also starts omo tasks, as the omo assistant does from Telegram. Type what you want done ("lets get started setting up remotion"): a sentence puts "Start an omo task" first, and a single word offers it after the new things. Enter on it asks where the task runs, with the task shown under a field that filters the places: places whose names, folders or omo messages share a word with the task come first ("Suggested"), then "New tmux session <name>" on this Mac and each connected machine (named after the task's first word that isn't filler such as "get started setting up", or what you type in the field), each machine's tmux sessions (a new omo window in that session's folder), and each running omo session (the task is handed to it). Enter runs `omo-sessions start --tmux <session> -- <task>` or `omo-sessions handoff <pane> <task>` on that machine and shows the pane with `omo-status show`; Esc goes back to the search with your text. A sentence of three words or more doesn't offer "Create tmux session", since it is a task, not a name.

Typing a name also offers "Create tmux session <name>" on this Mac and on each connected machine where no session has that name. When nothing else matches, the first of these comes first and is selected, so a name and Enter run `omo-status session <name> [machine]`: it starts the session in your home folder and attaches it in a space of its own, or shows the session if it already exists. Spaces in the name become dashes and only letters, digits, - and _ stay, since tmux turns `.` and `:` into `_` and omo-status splits on spaces. The row spins until the space attaches (about 3 seconds), and an error shows as a toast. The selection is one highlight that slides to the row you pick, by arrow keys or by moving the pointer (a list scrolling under a still pointer doesn't move it), and "needs you" marks pulse.

The cloned picker, sidebar chrome and layout don't get Herdr's later changes to the shipped ones. To pick those up, clone again from the new release and carry the tmux additions over. The picker's look is its own since 2026-11-15 (as the Agents tree, Changes and the layout largely are), so most of Herdr's changes to those won't merge by themselves: `herdr-tmux-update` then leaves our file alone and hands you the change to port (see below).

## Spaces

A tmux session can have a Herdr space of its own, named after it and running a tmux client attached to it. Its terminal then carries the session's omo status for Herdr's tab dots, sounds and toasts (the sidebar's tree shows the session's panes under the space's tabs instead).

- `omo-status show <pane>` (the panel and search use it) focuses the Herdr tab already on the pane's session. Without one, it opens a tab attached to the session in the space named after it, or makes that space in the pane's folder. It never moves a tmux client you have open to another session.
- Openings run one at a time (`show`, `open`, `spaces`), and each waits up to 5 s for its tmux client to attach before the next one looks. Two at once used to find no client each and open the session twice (a double click, or Telegram's "Open on Mac" with a click).
- `omo-status spaces`, or the layers button in the panel's header, makes a space for every tmux session running omo that no Herdr tab shows, closes the spaces it made for sessions that have ended, and puts you back where you were. The spaces it made are listed in `~/.local/state/omo-status/spaces`. It asks Herdr which tmux session each terminal is attached to, finds a space by its saved id before its name (so a renamed space is still found), and opens nothing if it can't read what Herdr already shows.
- In Herdr outside tmux, `owt` opens each workspace as a space of its own, named after its title. Herdr's sidebar nests it under the repo's space when that is open.
- After a logout or reboot each tab that showed tmux comes back with a shell: herdr-core gives a tab whose program was the tmux client a fresh shell, and a tab that ran `tma` is back at its own. `omo-status reattach` puts each back on its tmux session by typing `exec tmux attach-session -t '=<session>'` into it (`hc send`). It goes by which session each terminal showed at the last tmux-resurrect save (`~/.local/state/omo-status/herdr-tabs`, from `omo-status herdr-save`), or, for a fresh shell with none there, the session its space was made for. It types only into a shell with nothing running and only for a session no tab shows yet, and starts tmux first when none runs (`omo-sessions boot`, so tmux-continuum restores it). `tmux-context` runs it as Herdr starts, and `omo-sessions resurrect` (tmux-resurrect's restore hook) runs it before it reopens the omo sessions, so omo's space sync finds their sessions shown and opens no second tab.

A session attached in Ghostty and in Herdr at once is sized to whichever client you used last (tmux's default `window-size latest`).

### Tab mode

`omo-status tabs <session> on` gives each window of a tmux session a Herdr tab of its own (`<machine>/<session>` for one on a Herdr machine), in the session's space, in window order and named after the window's work, as the Agents list names it. Each tab carries its own window's omo status, so Herdr's tab dots, sounds and toasts are per agent, and the space shows its most urgent tab. `omo-status tabs <session> off` goes back to one tab on the session, keeping the tab you are on, and `omo-status tabs <session>` says which it is. `omo-status tabs --all on` puts every session on every machine in tab mode, those made later too (each gets its tabs when Herdr first shows it), and `omo-status tabs <session> off` then leaves one out; `omo-status tabs --all off` takes every session back to one tab. The sessions in tab mode are listed in `~/.local/state/omo-status/tab-sessions` (a name per line, `*` for every session, `-<name>` for one left out), so the mode outlives a restart. Every session on this Mac is in tab mode since 2026-11-18.
- A Herdr tab attached to a session in tab mode by other means (`tma`, a tab opened by hand) becomes the tab of the window it shows as it attaches, and a session Herdr doesn't show gets no space just for being in tab mode.

- A tab's tmux client is on a session of its own, `<session>~<window id>`: it is in a session group with the session, so it shares its windows, and keeps its own current window, the one its tab shows. Closing the tab ends that session (its client sets `destroy-unattached` as it attaches). The lists (`omo-status list`, `panes`, `sessions`, `jobs`, the Agents tree, search, the priority queue, `tmux-session-tint`, `omo-sessions` snapshots) leave these sessions out, since each would list every pane again. In Herdr, tmux's bar is empty, kept for its `:` prompt and messages, and the pane header names the tmux session in place of the program and folder (`pane-tmux`, from `tmux-context`'s shared "tmux.panes").
- tmux's hooks keep the tabs right (`omo-status tabs-sync`): a new window gets a tab in the background, a window that closes takes its tab with it, and a tab moved to another window (prefix n, or a window made in it) goes back to its own while the tab of the window it went to is shown.
- A tab you close stays closed until you show its pane (a click in the Agents tree or the panel, Cmd+J, the priority queue), which opens it again. A Herdr tab on the session itself, one `spaces` or a reattach opened, becomes the tab of the window it shows.
- Going to a tab sees the panes of its window and moves nothing, since a tab keeps its window. `where` follows the tab, so Files and Changes show the folder of the window you are on.
- After a logout, `herdr-save` notes each tab's window by its index (window ids start again when tmux-resurrect restores them) and `reattach` puts the tab back on that window.
- On a Herdr machine the tmux side runs there, sent along with `hc exec`, and Herdr's side here. The machine keeps its own list of sessions in tab mode, and its tmux hooks (`.tmux-server.conf`), which can't reach Herdr, note the session in `~/.local/state/omo-status/tabs/pending` and touch the tick beside it. The Mac's follow agent (`omo-status follow`) hears of it within a second and runs `omo-status tabs-pending <machine>`, which sets the tabs right here. A tab name follows the window's work on this Mac only; a machine's tabs keep the name they opened with.

## Attention

- The panel is an inbox: panes that need you, failed, finished, working or running something in the background, with idle ones folded into an "N idle" row that opens them.
- Cmd+J in the `netrunner` layout (`omo-tmux.next` in `keys.json`, `omo-status next` from a shell) shows the pane that most needs you: a question or a failure, then a finished turn, the longest waiting first. Looking at it clears it, so pressing again moves on.
- A tmux session holding several agents shows its most urgent state on its Herdr terminal, naming that pane and the queue behind it (`needs you · reoclo: which server? (+1 more)`); the Agents tree shows each pane's own state. Going to the session selects the pane that most needs you (a failure, a question, then a finished turn, the longest waiting first) and clears only that pane, so the session keeps its next state and the next visit goes to the next pane, until it falls to working, background or idle. A click on a panel row or board card that needs you goes to that pane; a click on one that doesn't goes to the session's most urgent pane instead. A click on a row of the Agents tree always goes to that pane. Herdr sends tmux no focus event, so going to a session's tab in Herdr is seen as a change of Herdr's focused terminal, within the panel's refresh.
- Herdr's agents list reads "<space> · <work>": `omo-status` names a session's Herdr tab after its most urgent pane's window (an `owt` title) or task, and sends it as the report's OSC 7501 `title`. Only tabs still called by their number, or by the label it set, change.
- omo sends no Stop hook while a background task (a terminal monitor, a child session) is live, so a finished turn could stay "working". `omo-notify` records each pane's transcript (`@omo_transcript`), and `omo-status list` reads the transcript's last `session-lifecycle` entry. A pane with terminal monitors, background commands, detached eval cells or child sessions still live goes to `background` (a purple dot on its tab), as does a pane with a job in `~/.omo/agent/schedule/pending/`; one waiting on nothing real goes to idle. omo's own plumbing in that entry (queued deliveries, goal timers, open questions) doesn't count.
- A question asked without holding the turn stays "needs you" when the turn ends; answering it, or a new prompt, moves the pane on.
- `omo-status` and `omo-notify` write a tmux option only when its value changes: they read what they compare in one call and put whatever changed in one more. With a dozen clients attached, a tmux call that sets options can hold the whole server for up to 0.4 s, and every pane stops redrawing meanwhile. What changes on nearly every pass lives in files under `~/.local/state/omo-status/` instead: the Herdr terminal last focused and when `omo-status` or the Agents tree last moved the focus itself (`herdr-focus`, `herdr-shown`), and what `list` last read of each pane (`seen/<pane>`).
- A pane can go without omo saying so: `owt done` kills its window, or the pane dies. tmux's `window-unlinked`, `pane-exited` and `after-kill-pane` hooks run `omo-status refresh`, which works out again each session still reporting an agent whose omo panes aren't the ones it counted last (`@omo_panes`, kept by every status update), so no session keeps reporting a pane that's gone. tmux's `pane-exited` doesn't name the session, so this is how refresh finds it: about 30 ms when nothing changed, where working out every session took 3 to 4 s.
- Spaces sync by themselves: omo's start hook runs `omo-status spaces --local` when Herdr is running, and tmux's `session-closed` hook closes the space of a session that ended. `--local` opens spaces only for this Mac's sessions; the layers button opens them for every machine's. A lock keeps two syncs from overlapping.
- tmux marks a client in a Herdr terminal focused even while its space is hidden, since Herdr sends it no focus-out, so `omo-status` doesn't count Herdr clients as looking at a pane. Going to a pane through the panel, search or Cmd+J clears it instead.

## Priority queue

The priority queue, `queue` (`mods/priority-queue`) at the top of the `netrunner` layout's right column (see Right column), lists what needs you, most urgent first, and opens by itself when something new does: a question, a failure, a finished turn or step, or a goal agent-state holds blocked. It closes when the last card goes, unless a card is pinned. Cmd+Shift+J, the status bar's `Queue N` chip (a pink dot while something asks you, green while only finished work waits) and the column's own close button toggle it.

- A card has the state (NEEDS YOU, FAILED or BLOCKED pulse; FINISHED, STEP DONE), the tmux session with its colour (see Session colours), another machine's name in yellow, when it joined the queue (a time of day, with the date when it isn't today, and how long ago; the full time on hover), the session's title and its project and window.
- A card with a question open counts down to when omo stops waiting on it: agent-state's `deadlineAt`, omo's `askUser.timeoutMinutes` (30 by default, at most 2 hours) from when it was asked. The clock says what happens then: CARRIES ON IN (the agent goes on with its own call) or, for a question asked as required, PAUSES IN (it leaves what the question gates undone and ends its turn). A session with a wake-up scheduled (agent-state's `resumeAt`) counts down to it as RESUMES IN. Under the clock a bar burns down from when the wait began, cyan, then yellow under half, then red under a fifth, with the clock pulsing in its last minute; netrunner's HUD draws it as a row of cells going dark. The soonest countdown also shows under the column's title. Typing in the question box in the pane gives you more time, which the queue doesn't see, and a card you answered or pinned stale has no clock.
- A question shows each question in Markdown with its options as buttons. A click on an option answers it; several questions or a multiple choice take picks, then Send answer. Comment opens a box, and while it is open a click on an option picks it instead of sending: Send then sends your picks with the text as omo's comment on the answer (a last `Comment:` paragraph, which the board extension in `omo-assistant` hands the question as its comment), or the text alone as an answer typed out. A finished turn or failure shows the "For you" part of the agent's last reply, and Full reply the whole of it. Mermaid fences draw as diagrams (`herdr-render`, cached with the preview's), other fences as code.
- Go to, or a click on a card's top line, shows the pane (`omo-status show`). Seen clears a finished turn or failure as looking at the pane does (`omo-status ack <pane>`, which sets it idle there, on any machine). Reply on any card sends the agent its next prompt, or a steer while it works. A card you answer or reply to turns grey (ANSWERED) and drops below the rest, with your answer under a spinner, until the agent takes it: its pane works again, or its question closes and it goes as a Seen would. agent-state hears of an answer a few seconds after the pane goes back to work, so until then what the session asked before your answer raises no card of its own (shared `omo.queue.answered` keeps when you last answered each session). A send that fails brings the card back with the error.
- The pin at the end of a card's top line keeps the card in the queue until you unpin it, in a Pinned group below the rest, lit pink. Its pane can move on (you look at it, press Seen, or the agent works again) and the card stays as it was when you pinned it. A pinned card doesn't count as needing you (no pulse, not in the chip's pink count), never opens the column, and keeps it from closing by itself. Answering or replying to it from the queue unpins it. Shared `omo.queue.pinned` keeps the pinned cards, at most 30.
- Which panes need you is omo-status's own state (`omo.list`), so a card goes as soon as you look at its pane, from anywhere, and the pane Herdr shows gets none. The questions, options and replies come from agent-state, matched by the omo session id in each pane's transcript name, and a session with a question open is a question card whatever its pane says (omo-status can call it finished once the turn that asked has ended). Answers go through agent-state's reply route to the session's board extension, the same path a Telegram reply takes. With agent-state out of reach the cards still come, from omo-status, and say what's missing.
- A hidden copy beside the status bar reads as `omo.list` changes and every 5 seconds, publishes shared `omo.queue`, and shows the column when a card arrives that it hasn't shown before; the column only draws. A read that fails keeps the cards there were and says why at the top of the column, and one that hasn't come back in a minute is left behind for a new one, so the queue can't stick on one bad read.

## Right column

The side panel and the priority queue share one column at the right of the `netrunner` layout (`right`), as an accordion: when both are open, one fills the column and the other folds to a 40 px bar above or below it, so they never sit side by side.

- The one you opened last expands and the other folds: a new queue card, Cmd+Shift+J or the `Queue N` chip expands the queue, and a side panel chip, Files or Changes, Cmd+Alt+B or a Ctrl+clicked file expands the side panel. A click on a bar expands its part.
- Closing the expanded part (its toggle, its own close button, or the queue emptying) hands the column back to the folded one. A bar's x closes its part without touching the other, and a folded queue whose last card goes closes by itself.
- The queue's bar reads PRIORITY QUEUE with how many cards need you (pink) or wait; the side panel's names its view (Files, Changes, MCP, omo in tmux, Jobs, PRDs, Reviews) and the folder of the tmux pane it follows.
- The column keeps the side panel's width while the panel is open, expanded or folded, so switching parts never resizes the terminal and tmux and omo don't redraw. The queue alone keeps its own width.
- `side-fit` does the arranging. The toggles still show and hide `panel` and `queue`; it compares what the layout shows with what it last arranged, folds or expands the other part, and shows or hides the bars (`queue-bar`, `panel-bar`, drawn by `side-fit` itself) and the column. Both parts fill the column by weight, and a width Herdr kept from when they were columns of their own is dropped.

## Narrow windows

`side-fit` keeps the terminal wide when the window narrows, as in a tiled or half-screen Herdr. Two copies run in 0 px blocks of the `netrunner` layout: `fit-width` across the work column measures the window, and `fit-height` beside the terminal measures its row.

- With room for everything (at least 720 px of terminal beside it, about 80 columns), the sidebar and the right column dock as before.
- Without it, the right column floats over the terminal at the right edge instead of squeezing it, as one window with its accordion inside, and so does the sidebar when docking it would. The terminal keeps its width under them, so tmux and omo don't redraw. They are drawers: a click on the terminal closes whatever floats, as its toggle would (a faint backdrop over the terminal takes the click). The floating column narrows so at least 160 px of terminal stays clear to click.
- Narrower, once less than 420 px of terminal shows beside them, the sidebar folds to its rail, then the right column closes, so the smallest window is nearly all terminal. Widening brings back what folded, expanded and folded as it was, unless you opened or closed it yourself in between.
- Everything that opens or switches the side panel and the queue still works: the status chips, Files and Changes, Cmd+Alt+B, Cmd+Shift+J, a Ctrl+clicked file, a new queue card. The right column keeps its slot in the layout (`min` 0, at 0 px while it floats), and side-fit copies what that slot shows into a window of its own, since Herdr's layout commands never reach a window's own nodes. The sidebar's toggle runs `side-fit.sidebar`, which docks the sidebar or floats it as the width allows.
- Docked widths are remembered (shared `side.fit`: the side panel's and the queue's, and `sidebar.width` for the sidebar), so docking again restores them. Dragging the column's edge sets the width of the part it is sized for.

## Session colours

Every tmux session has a colour of its own, so you can tell sessions apart at a glance in the Agents tree, on this Mac and on a remote server: the session's rows get a wash of it, and the priority queue's cards name the session in it. Panes keep the terminal's own background. `.local/bin/tmux-session-tint` takes the colours from a fixed list of ten (blue, orange, teal, magenta, lime, violet, gold, cyan, coral, green; no yellow, which marks other machines, and no pure red, the focused pane's edge), ordered so that each is 115 to 175 degrees round the wheel from the one before, and gives them out in the order the Agents tree shows the sessions, so neighbours always contrast. The Agents tree hands each machine's tmux that order (`tmux-session-tint --order <names>`, kept in `~/.local/state/omo-status/session-order`) whenever it changes, by a drag or a session coming or going, and takes the colours it prints at once; sessions the tree doesn't show follow in tmux's order. A session coming, going or moving can recolour the ones after it. It keeps the colour in the session option `@tint_color`. `omo-status tints` lists each session's colour, this machine's and each Herdr machine's, which the hidden `omo-tmux` copy publishes every 15 seconds as shared `tmux.tints` for the Agents tree and the priority queue.

## Board

Cmd+Shift+B in the `netrunner` layout, or the screen button in the Agents list's header, opens the board (`mods/omo-board`) as a dialog centered over the dimmed window, 80% by 78% of it, in the netrunner colours with its own pink neon frame (`frame: "none"` in its `mod.json`); Esc, the ESC keycap or the same key again closes it. It shows every omo session on every machine the omo board knows, read every five seconds from agent-state (`127.0.0.1:4747`, through the omo board's tunnel). Sessions sit in five columns: Needs you (an open question or a blocked goal, oldest first), Working, Background (the turn is over but a build, watcher or child session still runs), Done (finished in the last day) and Idle. A card shows the session's title, its machine, project and tmux target, the question waiting or the "For you" part of its last report, its to-do progress, and a dot for how recently it was active: hot under an hour, warm under a day, dim after that. A click runs `omo-status open`, which shows the session in its Herdr space, or a new one, server sessions included. Sessions not in tmux can't be shown from here.

Press `/` on the omo Board to search it: typing filters every column by session title, project, machine, tmux target or message (every word must match), Enter opens the first match, and Esc clears the search, then closes it, before closing the Board.

Telegram's "Open on Mac" button (omo-assistant) runs the same `omo-status open` on the Mac through the board, and brings Herdr forward. `omo-presence` tells omo-assistant whether you are at the Mac (Herdr in front, screen unlocked, keyboard or mouse used in the last five minutes); while you are, it keeps a session's questions and errors off Telegram, since Herdr alerts you.

## Preview

`mods/preview` shows a file beside your terminal:

- Markdown rendered, with a Rendered/Source toggle. A file with fenced code or Mermaid is drawn in parts: each fence in a read-only code view with highlighting, each Mermaid fence as a diagram, the prose between them as before.
- HTML as WebKit lays the page out, at about the pane's width (CSS, layout and images; nothing on the page reacts to clicks), with Source beside it; Herdr's own HTML view when it can't be drawn.
- Images fitted to the pane, never enlarged at first. The zoom bar under an image zooms out (-), in (+), to actual size (0), to fit (F) and to the pane's width (W), keeping the middle of the view in place, and scrolling pans a zoomed image. The zoom keys are plain keys, since Herdr keeps Cmd+=, Cmd+- and Cmd+0 for its own zoom. HEIC and TIFF are converted first.
- Text and code with line numbers and highlighting, in your terminal font when it is an installed family (the code view can't find "JetBrainsMono Nerd Font", so it uses JetBrains Mono). Herdr's code view knows JavaScript and TypeScript (as JavaScript), Python, Ruby, Go, Rust, Swift, C, C++, Java, Scala, PHP, Lua, Perl, Bash, Make, CSS, HTML, SQL, JSON, YAML and TOML; other languages show as plain text. Compact JSON (three lines or fewer) is pretty-printed. Files over 1 MB or 20,000 lines show their start, and say so.
- Word, RTF and similar documents through `textutil`; PDFs page by page (the first 20 pages); Keynote, Numbers, PowerPoint and Excel files through Quick Look.
- Folders as a list you can click through, and anything else as an info card.

The header copies the path (Cmd+Shift+C), reveals the file in Finder (Cmd+Shift+R), opens it in its default app (Cmd+O) and reloads it (Cmd+R). It also reloads by itself when the file changes. For text, code, Markdown, HTML files and folders, Edit (Cmd+E) opens nvim in a terminal below the preview, in the file's folder, so the preview follows each save. nvim starts through your interactive login shell, so it has what your terminals have; started bare, it lacks `XDG_CONFIG_HOME` from `.zshrc` and the nvim config's WezTerm theme sync fails.

Left and right (the arrow keys, or the arrows at the end of the header's second line) move to the previewable files before and after the shown one in its folder, in the Files tree's order, with its place as "3 / 12" between the arrows. Back and the Rendered/Source choice stay, and each image opens fitted again. Folders, and files whose kind the preview would have to guess, are skipped. `keys.json` can bind `preview.prev`, `preview.next`, `preview.zoom-in`, `preview.zoom-out`, `preview.zoom-fit`, `preview.zoom-width` and `preview.zoom-actual`.

`herdr-render` (`.local/bin`, run with bun) draws the HTML pages and Mermaid diagrams with the system WebKit (`Bun.WebView`) into the preview cache: a page in tiles of 2,000 CSS pixels (a taller picture can't be drawn), a diagram in the dark Mermaid theme on the Herdr theme's background. The first diagram installs the `mermaid` package into `~/.cache/herdr-render`, which takes about a minute and needs the network; later ones take under a second, and each diagram and page is drawn once per version. A diagram Mermaid can't parse shows its source and the error.

A file on another Herdr machine previews too: Files sends it with its machine, the preview lists its folder and reads its size and time there, and fetches the file (up to 8 MB, the most `hc exec` passes, or the first 1 MB of a larger text file) into the cache, where it is drawn like a file here. The machine shows in yellow at the start of the header's second line. A remote file has no Edit or Reveal, Open opens the fetched copy, and the preview checks the file every 5 seconds instead of watching its folder.

Focus mode (Cmd+Shift+F, or the expand button at the end of the header) opens the file in a modal over most of the window, for reading Markdown or looking at an image or PDF closely: larger text, a wider reading column for Markdown and HTML, and images fitted to the bigger space. It works from the Files panel's preview and from a split or tab, keeps the Rendered/Source choice, and has no Edit. Esc, Cmd+Shift+F, its close button or a click beside it closes it.

Open it with `herdr-preview <path>`, a split beside the terminal you run it in (`--tab` for a tab of its own), or click a path in a tmux pane (see Clicking paths and links below): from Herdr every file opens in the preview, code and HTML included. Ctrl+click anywhere on a Markdown link or image in a pane, `[label](target)` or `![alt](target)`, opens its target. omo draws a link as `label (target)`, and Ctrl+click on the label opens the target too; omo draws an image link as its alt text only, so it has no path to click. Outside Herdr, Ctrl+click keeps its old routes. Herdr has no setting for what its own Cmd+click does with file paths.

### Clicking paths and links

In a tmux pane, `tmux-open-path` opens the path or web link under the pointer. Inside Herdr on macOS that is **Option+click**: Herdr turns Ctrl+click into a right-click (its terminal menu, or a plain right-click when right-clicks go to the program) and handles Cmd+click itself: it opens a clicked path with macOS `open`, in the file's default app (Ghostty for scripts and files without an extension), so neither reaches tmux. Outside Herdr (Ghostty) it is Ctrl+click, as before.

- What a click names, first match wins: an OSC 8 hyperlink under it (read from the pane's line with its escapes, which tmux keeps, so its exact target counts); a Markdown link around it; the text inside the nearest quotes or backticks; the run of text drawn in one style around it (omo draws inline code in its own colour, without the backticks); the words around it, as many as name a real path, longest first; omo's `label (target)`. So a path with spaces opens from a click anywhere on it, quoted, in an omo code span, with `\ ` escapes or bare. Only a path wrapped onto a second terminal line can't, since tmux reports one line.
- A web link (`http`, `https`) opens in the default browser, plain or as a Markdown, omo or OSC 8 link.
- In the `netrunner` layout, a file opens in the side panel's preview and a folder in its Files list. `tmux-open-path` leaves the request in `~/.local/state/omo-status/open/request.json`; the hidden `tmux-context` copy picks it up, sets `files.open` or `files.root` and shows the panel. A clicked folder stays in the Files list while that pane stays in its folder; the back arrow in the Files header returns to the pane's folder.
- A `path:line` opens the file without going to the line: the preview's Edit opens nvim at the top.
- The Files header's folder button opens the folder it shows in Finder; the preview's folder button reveals the file in Finder, selected in its folder.
- On a remote server the click runs on the server, which has no Herdr panel to open, so it keeps its old routes there.

Watch out:

- Herdr's `image()` only loads files inside `~/.config/herdr-app`, so images, converted images and PDF pages are copied into `~/.config/herdr-app/preview-cache`, outside this repo and pruned after 7 days.
- Herdr's Markdown view draws no images: an image in Markdown shows as an "Image: alt" link that opens it in the preview. Bold and italic showed as plain text in testing.
- Code doesn't wrap, so columns stay aligned; long lines scroll sideways.
- An open preview pane keeps running its old code after `main.js` changes, even after a layout switch: close and reopen it.

## Files and Changes

Herdr's Files and Changes panels follow the focused terminal's folder, and for a tmux client that is wherever `tmux attach` started, not the pane you are working in: a session opened in a repo's main checkout kept showing it while omo worked in an owt worktree. The `netrunner` layout uses `files-tmux` and `changes-tmux` instead, which show the folder of the tmux pane the client shows, with the `tmux-context` strip above them. The tab bar's Files and Changes buttons open them as before.

- `omo-status where <terminal>` finds the tmux client a Herdr terminal runs (`hc get <terminal> --json` `.foreground.pid` is its `#{client_pid}`) and lists its session's panes, with omo found by its process as `omo-status list` does. For a terminal on another Herdr machine it runs through `hc exec` and takes its own function along, so the server's copy of `omo-status` doesn't need updating. A terminal running no tmux client gets nothing back, and the panels show Herdr's own folder.
- `tmux-context` publishes that pane's folder as the shared value `tmux.where`, which the two panels read; until it has looked at a newly focused terminal they show "Finding the tmux pane…" rather than the client's folder. It looks again when Herdr's focus moves, when tmux changes pane, window or session (hooks in `.tmux.conf` touch `~/.local/state/omo-status/where/tick`, which it watches), and every 3 seconds, which catches a `cd` and a remote server's sessions, whose hooks can't reach the Mac.
- The strip shows the folder (an owt workspace by its name, with its `owt title` beside it) and the repo; the branch with what is to push and pull (↑ ↓), how far it is ahead of and behind the default branch, and the uncommitted files; the Linear issue the branch names; the branch's PR with its state, review and checks (`gh pr view`, read again each minute); and the most urgent omo agent in the session working in that repo, which a click shows (`omo-status show`). A folder on another machine gets a yellow machine label.
- Clicking the folder's name (or `tmux-context.pick`, which `keys.json` can bind) lists the session's folders with their agents. Picking one pins the panels to that pane, whatever tmux shows, until you pick "Follow the active pane"; a pink pin marks it. Pins are kept per Herdr terminal in the shared value `tmux.pins`.
- Linear links come from `~/.config/herdr-app/linear.json`, which maps each Linear workspace to its team keys: `{ "<workspace>": ["ABC", "XYZ"] }`. It stays out of this repo and isn't linked. An issue of a team it doesn't list copies its id instead of opening.
- Changes has three views. Uncommitted is Herdr's: the work tree against the last commit. Branch is everything the branch changes since it left the default branch (`origin/HEAD`, else `main` or `master`, from their merge-base), uncommitted work and new files included. Commits lists the commits that aren't on the default branch, and opening one shows its whole patch. An owt worktree opens on Branch and anything else on Uncommitted; a view you pick stays for that repo while Herdr runs.
- An open file shows its `path:line` (its first change) with Preview (a split with the preview mod; this Mac only), Edit at the line (nvim in a terminal below, started through your login shell as the preview mod does) and Copy path. An open commit can copy its hash. A submodule (from `.gitmodules`) shows git's summary of it, or what changed inside it when that summary is empty (new files), and offers only Copy path. `keys.json` (or `herdr-app do changes-tmux.<command>`) reaches the same: `uncommitted`, `branch` and `commits` pick the view, `preview`, `edit` and `copy` act on what is open.
- A mod pane opened beside a tmux client, such as that preview, keeps the folder its tab's client showed: the strip, Files, Changes and the status bar stay on the worktree instead of jumping to the folder Herdr has for the space.
- In Files, a click on a file on this Mac opens it in the preview mod in the same place (the layout's `files` slot is a stack of `files-tree`, `files-preview` and `files-edit`; Files hands the file over in the shared value `files.open`). Markdown renders, "Files" or Esc goes back to the tree. The preview's Edit (Cmd+E) runs nvim in the panel too: the `files-edit` block shows the terminal named `files-nvim`, which runs `.local/bin/herdr-files-editor`. It starts on the first Edit and stays; the preview types the folder and file into it (`hc send`), it opens nvim there, and when nvim quits (`:q`) it writes `~/.local/state/herdr-files/closed`, which brings the preview back, reloaded. While nvim is open, Edit shows it again rather than opening another file in it. Files on another machine keep Herdr's read-only view, as the preview reads this Mac's files only. Moving to another tab or folder puts the tree back; an open nvim keeps running.
- The editor terminal never exits on purpose: when a terminal shown in a layout block ends, Herdr removes the block and rewrites the layout file, through the link, into this repo. If `files-nvim` is killed while it shows, check `git diff layouts/netrunner.json` and restore the file. Without the block, Edit opens nvim below the preview, as outside the panel.
- Herdr's Commit button is gone from Changes: it ran `git add -A` and committed everything in the repo shown. Commit from the terminal or ask the agent.
- The pane header (`pane-tmux`) shows the folder of the tmux pane each client shows, from the shared value `tmux.panes` that `tmux-context` fills for every tmux client in the shown tab. The status bar (`workspace-status-tmux`) shows that pane's branch, from `tmux.where`. The hidden `tmux-context` beside the status bar keeps both right with the panel closed, from the moment Herdr starts.
- Agents are only looked for in the tmux session the client shows.

## MCP

The status bar's MCP chip counts the MCP servers of the omo session in the tmux pane the focused client shows (or the first omo pane of that tmux session), with a yellow dot when one needs you. A click opens the MCP panel in the side panel, a third view beside Files and Changes; another click closes it. Herdr's tab bar has no button for it.

- The chip reads once the focus has stayed on a pane for 0.8 s, so passing through panes reads none of them, and again every two minutes; the panel's refresh button reads at once. `omo-status mcp` keeps what it found in each transcript (`~/.local/state/omo-status/mcp/`) and reads only what was added since, so a long session costs a full read once.
- `omo-status mcp <pane> [machine]` works the list out as omo does when a session starts or reloads: your `~/.omo/agent/mcp.json`, then the project's Claude `.mcp.json` (only when `importConfigs` has `"claude"`) and `.omo/mcp.json`, both only in a trusted project; omo's bundled `context7` and `grep_app` unless configured by name. On another machine it runs there, as `where` does.
- Each server shows where it comes from (user, project, claude, omo), how it runs, how many tools it listed last (omo's `mcp-cache.json`), its sign-in, a problem omo logged in the last day, and the session's own latest call to it since its config was read, from its transcript, with omo's error when it failed.
- omo keeps live connection state in its process, so a server the session hasn't called since shows only what its config says. The panel also says when the config was read, warns when an `mcp.json` changed after that (`/reload` picks it up), and names servers an untrusted project's `.omo/mcp.json` or an unimported `.mcp.json` would add.

## PRDs

The PRDs panel, a view of the side panel beside Jobs, shows which Boxmarshall Studio PRD each omo pane on this Mac is working on and how far it has come. The status bar's `PRDs N` chip opens and closes it, with a pink dot when Studio can't be read.

- The link comes from `omo-work`: an agent that runs `omo-work bind <task>` binds its omo session to a Studio task, and a task belongs to a PRD through its requirement line. `.local/bin/studio-prds` finds each pane running omo (`omo-status list-local`, or the rows given with `--rows`), its session from the pane's transcript (`@omo_transcript`), the session's client, kind (billed or in-house) and task from `~/.local/state/omo-work/sessions/<id>`, and asks Studio's `get_task_prds` for those tasks' PRDs in one call. It prints JSON: the PRDs, each with its panes, and the panes with no PRD.
- Each PRD is a card: its client in the accent colour with its status beside it, its title, version and urgency with its date (delivered, else its target, red once overdue or due today and in the working colour within three days, else when it started) and Read, Studio and Linear buttons (Linear when it has been copied there), a bar with one segment per requirement line (green done, blue in progress, grey open; each has the line as its tooltip), how many requirement lines and tasks are done, and the panes working on it, most urgent first, with their omo state, how long they have had it and the requirement their task is for. A click on a pane shows it (`omo-status show <pane> exact`).
- Client work on no PRD comes next: panes whose session omo-work counts as a client's but whose task is on no PRD, or that have no task bound, grouped by client with a Billed or In-house badge. Each says which: no task bound (`omo-work bind <task>` fixes it), or its task's title and that it is on no PRD. Panes that aren't client work fold into one row at the end.
- Read, or a click on the title, opens the whole PRD in a modal over the window: `studio-prds prd <id>` fetches its current version from Studio (`get_prd`), writes it as Markdown to `~/.local/state/studio-prds/prds/<client> - <title>.md` (title, client, status, version, urgency, a link to Studio, then the body, with each requirement line Studio counts as done ticked; files older than a week go), and the preview mod shows it in focus mode (`openOverlay` with props `{file, zen: true}`), rendered, with Source beside it. Esc closes it; the arrow keys step to other PRDs read before. `prds-tmux.read` (`herdr-app do prds-tmux.read`, or a key in `keys.json`) reads the PRD of the pane you are in, else the first one listed. Studio opens the PRD in the browser.
- Here shows only the PRDs of the tmux session you are in, and marks your pane; All shows every one.
- A hidden copy beside the status bar runs `studio-prds` when the omo panel's shared `omo.list` changes, handing it those rows (`--rows`) so the panes aren't listed a second time (about 85 ms a read instead of 370), and every 15 seconds; it publishes shared `studio.prds` and republishes the pane you are in when the focus moves. A failed Read, Studio or Linear says so in red for 10 seconds, or until clicked. `studio-prds` asks Studio again only when the set of bound tasks changes or its last answer is a minute old, and keeps the last answer in `~/.local/state/studio-prds/last.json` while Studio can't be reached.
- Studio access is a read-only API key, "Herdr PRD panel (read only)", in the login Keychain as `boxmarshall-studio-herdr`. To replace it: `security add-generic-password -U -a "$USER" -s boxmarshall-studio-herdr -w '<key>'`. `STUDIO_ORIGIN` and `STUDIO_KEY` point `studio-prds` at another Studio.
- Only this Mac's panes: omo-work's bindings live on the machine the session runs on.

## Reviews

The Reviews panel, a view of the side panel beside Jobs and PRDs, lists the pull requests of the repo of the tmux pane you are in, on GitHub or Gitea, with omo's reviews of them. The status bar's `Reviews N` chip counts the open PRs in view and opens and closes it, with a dot in the working colour while a review runs and a pink one when a repo can't be read, a review failed or an automatic post was refused. The design is in `docs/specs/2026-11-20-pr-review-pane-design.md`.

- `.local/bin/pr-review` (Bun, its code in `.local/bin/pr-review.d/`) does the work and the mod only draws, as `studio-prds` and `prds-tmux` do. Every command prints one JSON value, and `pr-review` alone lists them. Its state is in `~/.local/state/pr-review/`: one file per review, with its log, omo's events and the diff beside it, the modes, and a 30 second cache of each repo's PR list.
- The forge comes from the `origin` remote. A host that matches a `tea` login (its address or its SSH host) is Gitea, called with that login's token. Any other host `gh` holds a token for is GitHub. No hostname is written in this repo. A repo with no login shows the command that adds one (`gh auth login`, `tea login add`).
- Review on a PR's row starts omo's review in the background (`pr-review review <dir> <number>`). It fetches the PR's head into a throwaway worktree under `~/.local/state/pr-review/work/`, hands omo the diff with each line's number in the new file, and lets it read the rest of the checkout with read, grep, find and ls only: no shell, no edits, no memory, no child sessions. A run gets 20 minutes, and the worktree goes when it ends. `PR_REVIEW_MODEL` picks the model. The row shows the phase and the elapsed time, then the findings per severity (blocker, major, minor, nit), Stale once the PR's head has moved, or Failed with the reason.
- Open shows a review in a dialog over the window: its verdict (good, changes or questions), the summary, and the findings grouped by file, each with a checkbox. Blockers and majors start ticked. Post sends the ticked ones to the PR as one review from your own account. It is always a comment, never an approval or a request for changes. A finding on a line of the diff becomes an inline comment (with GitHub's apply-able suggestion when it has one), any other goes into the review's body, and the summary goes with the first post. Posted findings grey out and can't be posted twice. Esc closes the dialog.
- A PR can have several reviews: omo's, each agent's, and your own. The dialog's header switches between the ten newest.
- Write starts a review of your own (`pr-review manual`), and the dialog's Diff tab lets you click a line of the new file and write a comment on it (`pr-review add`). You can add your own comments to any review, and remove them until they are posted.
- Merged adds the PRs merged in the last 30 days, for reviews after the fact. A merged PR is diffed from where it branched, not against today's base branch. Both forges take comments on merged PRs.
- Each repo has a mode, and a PR can override it: manual, semi (the default) or auto. In manual, agents review only when you ask. In semi, agents review on their own and every review waits for you in the panel. In auto, a review's blockers and majors post as soon as it is done, whether an agent submitted it or you pressed Review. Modes are kept in `~/.local/state/pr-review/modes.json`, out of this repo.
- Agents use the same tool: `pr-review context <dir> <number>` gives them the PR, its mode and the numbered diff, and `pr-review submit <dir> <number> --name <agent>` stores their review, which shows in the panel under their name. The `pr-review` skill (`.config/omo/skills/pr-review`) and the "Pull request reviews" section of `.config/ai/AGENTS.md` tell them when and how.
- When there is nothing to list, the panel says why in the middle of the list, with a ringed icon, a title, a sentence and a way on: Reading pull requests (spinning); No tmux pane in focus; No open (or merged) pull requests, with Show merged (or Show open); Reviews run on this Mac only, for a pane on another machine. Errors are red: Not a git repository and No origin remote (with Show the whole session in Here), Not signed in (naming the login command), Repository not found and Access denied for an API 404, 401 or 403, and Couldn't read pull requests for anything else, each with Try again where trying again can help. With several repos listed, one that can't be read gets the same words in a compact red card under its folder, and a failed action or a read that fails with a list already shown is a red banner above the list that a click dismisses.
- Here shows the repo of the pane you are in, All every repo open in a pane of the tmux session. For a pane on another machine `pr-review` runs there, and its reviews run and are read there, so that machine needs bun, omo, the dotfiles' `pr-review` and a login for its repos' forge (`gh`, or a tea login for Gitea).
- A hidden copy beside the status bar runs `pr-review list` and publishes shared `pr.reviews` (`{data, problem, busy}`) for the panel and the chip. It reads again when the pane, Here / All (`pr.reviews.scope`) or Open / Merged (`pr.reviews.filter`) changes, after each action, every 60 seconds, and every 3 seconds while a review runs. The dialog rereads its review file every 3 seconds while the review runs.
- `reviews-tmux.open` (`herdr-app do reviews-tmux.open`, or a key in `keys.json`) opens the newest review of the first PR listed that has one.
- Tests: `cd .local/bin/pr-review.d && bun test --timeout 20000 ./diff.test.ts ./forge.test.ts ./review.test.ts ./post.test.ts`.

## Alfred

The Alfred tab of the right column is the Telegram chat with Alfred, the omo assistant on the server (`~/Projects/Applications/omo-assistant`, README "Desk"), so a message to Alfred, a reply to an agent or a press of an agent's button doesn't need the phone. The tab bar's `Alfred N` chip (after Files and Changes) opens and closes it and counts Alfred's messages you haven't seen there, leaving out the ones already answered (a 👍 from their session, a button used, or your reply to them); its number turns yellow and pulses while a message has buttons waiting on you (a session's choices, a retro review, a Claude sign-in), cyan while Alfred works and red when the board can't be read. Cmd+Shift+A (`alfred.open`) opens the tab and puts the keyboard in its box. A message from Alfred while the tab is hidden posts a toast that opens it.

- It is the same chat as on the phone. The bridge copies everything it does in the chat to the omo board hub, edits, buttons, reactions and deletions included, and your own messages from Telegram (marked You · Telegram). What you send here goes into Telegram first as a quiet message starting with 🖥, so the phone's chat stays whole, then to Alfred as if typed there: commands like `/status` or `/blocked` work too. It shows as You · Herdr, with a Sending row until the bridge has it.
- Agents' messages (questions, errors, progress, posts) carry their machine and project. Reply on one points the box at it, and what you send then reaches that session, as a Telegram reply would. Their buttons work here as on the phone, and the answer Telegram would show as a toast shows above the box. A button answered on the phone is refused here, so nothing is answered twice. Link buttons open in the browser.
- Messages read as Telegram draws them: bold, italic, code and quotes. Photos, voice notes and files show as a chip with the name and length. All of the 200 read at the start scroll, up to 400 kept, but only the messages near the screen are built, the rest standing in as spacers of the height they last measured, since Herdr renders the tab again whenever its live data changes. The chat stays at its end until you scroll or press in it, and goes back to following once you are at the end again or a message arrives, down to the bottom of a tall one.
- The hub is reached through this Mac's board tunnel (`OMO_BOARD_URL` in `~/.config/omo-board/env`, else `127.0.0.1:7700`) with the dashboard's board token (`BOARD_CONTROL_TOKEN` in `~/.config/agent-state/server.env`). The tab and a hidden copy beside the status bar each keep a long poll open on it (a read waits up to 25 s for news). The hidden copy publishes shared `alfred.chat` (`{loaded, unread, waiting, typing, problem}`) for the chip, and shared `alfred.read` is the newest message of Alfred's the tab has shown.

## Herdr updates

`herdr-tmux-update` carries our copies onto a new Herdr: it keeps Herdr's own versions we copied from in `base/`, merges what Herdr changed since into `picker-tmux`, `workspace-chrome-tmux`, `agents-tmux`, `files-tmux`, `changes-tmux`, `pane-tmux`, `workspace-status-tmux`, `tabs-tmux` and the `netrunner` layout with `git merge-file`, and checks the mods one by one. Each merge is tried on a copy first, since our files are live and one left with `<<<<<<<` blocks would stop loading: a clean merge replaces ours and records Herdr's version in `base/`. When Herdr's change runs into ours, our file and its base stay as they are and `base/pending/` gets `<name>.diff` (what Herdr changed) and `<name>.merged` (the attempted merge, conflicts and all). Port what applies by hand, check the mod with `herdr-app check`, then run `herdr-tmux-update --ported <name>` to record Herdr's version as the base; until then every run reports it again, with any later Herdr changes added to the diff. It exits 1 while anything is left to port. Run it when you mean to merge, not as a check: it writes Herdr's clean changes into our copies and records them in `base/`.

## Remote Herdr machines

A server added as a Herdr machine (`hc machine add <machine>`, with herdr-core installed there) has its tmux sessions work like the Mac's:

- The panel, search and Cmd+J list its panes as `<machine>/<session>`. `omo-status` on the Mac reads them by running the server's own `omo-status` through `hc exec --on <machine>`.
- Opening one shows the Herdr tab already on its session, or makes a space for it on the server. The layers button makes spaces for its sessions too; the automatic syncs don't, so no server session is attached from Herdr, and resized, unless you ask.
- On the server, `~/.local/bin/omo-status` links to the dotfiles clone's, and `.tmux-server.conf` runs its `sync` and `focus` hooks. The server's own `~/.local/bin/omo-notify`, which is not in this repo, calls `omo-status set` on every omo event. A tmux client in a Herdr terminal there gets the same OSC 7501 report as on the Mac and no `omo:` title.
- Its ntfy push carries a `herdr` tag when a Herdr terminal shows the session, and `omo-remote` on the Mac then leaves the sound and banner to Herdr.

A session attached from mosh and from Herdr at once is sized to the client used last.

## Watch out

- Herdr hot-reloads a mod when it sees the mod's files change, and it doesn't see edits made through the link. After editing `mods/omo-tmux/`, switch layouts away and back (Cmd+Shift+L) or restart Herdr. Check a mod with `herdr-app check ~/.config/herdr-app/mods/omo-tmux`.
- Herdr writes `gpui-kit.d.ts` and `jsconfig.json` into the mod folder, through the link. They are gitignored.
- Changing settings in Herdr's Settings window may replace the `settings.json` link with a plain file. If `ls -l ~/.config/herdr-app/settings.json` no longer shows a link, copy the changes here and run `setup-mac.sh links`.
- Herdr runs tmux without a UTF-8 locale, so the panel runs `tmux -u` and splits fields on `|^|`: tmux prints control characters as `_`.
